About the New Seven Wonders

The New Seven Wonders of the World is a list announced in 2007 after a worldwide poll. These places span several continents and represent extraordinary achievements in architecture, engineering, and art.

Explore the seven wonders

1. Great Wall of China

Location: China

A vast system of walls and fortifications built over many centuries to protect Chinese states and empires. Its routes cross mountains, deserts, and plains.

2. Petra

Location: Jordan

An ancient city carved into rose-colored rock. Petra became an important center of trade for the Nabataeans, who built an impressive system of water channels and reservoirs.

3. Christ the Redeemer

Location: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

This statue of Jesus Christ stands atop Mount Corcovado, overlooking Rio de Janeiro. It is recognized around the world as a symbol of the city.

4. Machu Picchu

Location: Peru

A 15th-century Inca settlement high in the Andes Mountains. Its carefully fitted stone buildings and terraces sit among dramatic mountain scenery.

5. Chichén Itzá

Location: Yucatán, Mexico

A major Maya city known for its large pyramid, El Castillo, as well as temples, observatories, and a great ball court.

6. The Colosseum

Location: Rome, Italy

An enormous ancient Roman amphitheater that hosted public events and spectacles. Its arches and tiers remain a striking example of Roman engineering.

7. Taj Mahal

Location: Agra, India

A white-marble mausoleum commissioned by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of Mumtaz Mahal. The complex is celebrated for its symmetry, gardens, and detailed craftsmanship.

Quick facts

  • The wonders are located across Asia, Europe, and South America.
  • They include ancient cities, monuments, and a modern statue.
  • The Great Pyramid of Giza is an honorary wonder in the New7Wonders campaign.

Why are there different lists of world wonders?

The ancient world had its own list of seven wonders, created by Greek writers. Modern lists use different criteria and reflect different eras, so the phrase “Seven Wonders” can refer to more than one collection.
